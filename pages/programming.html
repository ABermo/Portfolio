My Programming History

2016

How it started

My first introduction to programming was similar to most, Scratch. I was a member of a CoderDojo where I got to make different games using Scratch, I did this for two years before moving onto beginner's Python.
To say I was confused, doesn't even begin to accurately describe how I felt, a few weeks into it the end of term came and I never went back.

I wasn't introduced back into programming until I got to do a Web Development course with CTYI and later an App Design course, here I learnt the very basics of HTML and again I was hooked.
It felt so cool being able to create websites and over the next few years I tried to learn Python a few times, all of which ended unsuccessfully.

2019

Leaving Cert Computer Science

After a back and forth relationship with coding and programming, I went into 4th Year of the Leaving Cert and one of the options for a choice subject was Computer Science, and in the second half of the first term I got a 6 week trial period of the subject.
It was entirely in Scratch as the vast majority of people had no coding experience before this, obviously with my vast experience, I nailed it!

Computer Science shot straight up to my number one choice for Leaving Cert options and I began it the following September, it was here where I was finally successful in learning Python.
It was by far the subject I put the most time and effort into, and the effort paid off, achieving a H1 grade, including a perfect practical section.
My project for the subject was a Data Analytics one, this is what sparked my interest in Data Science and what ultimately led me to picking it for my University Degree.

2025

TU850, Data Science and AI

From my enjoyment of my computer science project, once the CAO options opened up I immediately put TU850 as my top choice, and it never moved.
I chose it from my enjoyment of the data science project and I was also strongly interested in the AI aspect due to its quickly advancing progress and development.

This course has taught me many more programming languages, expanding my knowledge from just Python, to being proficient in C, SQL, PHP, JavaScript and very comfortable using HTML and CSS.
Despite this expansion to my knowledge, Python still remains my preferred language due to its endless possibilities with all the different libraries.

2026

Today

It's really cool being able to look back and see how I went from making small Scratch games, to Python number guessing games, and now making AI models for games such as Blackjack.
